Property Acquisition & Underwriting

Good deals are made at the purchase, not at the sale. We source properties through direct outreach, agent relationships, probate and estate channels, and longstanding local networks across Malden, Greater Boston, and Middlesex County.

Every candidate property runs through the same underwriting model — conservative after-repair value, realistic holding costs, contingency reserves, and a downside scenario. If a deal doesn't survive the pessimistic case, we don't make the offer.

Questions

FREQUENTLY ASKED

What types of properties does Trinity buy?
Primarily single-family homes, small multifamily (2–4 units), and select condominiums in Malden, Greater Boston, and Middlesex County. We focus on value-add properties where renovation can meaningfully increase both livability and market value.
How fast can you close on a property?
For cash purchases with clear title, typically 14–21 days. We can also accommodate longer timelines if you need time to relocate or coordinate with an estate — the schedule is yours to set.
What does the rehab actually include?
Scope varies by property, but typically covers structural repairs, roof, mechanicals (electrical, plumbing, HVAC), kitchens, baths, flooring, paint, and exterior/curb appeal. Everything is documented in a line-item scope of work before work begins.
How is investor capital secured?
Private lending is secured by a first-lien mortgage on the acquired property, recorded at closing, with terms documented in writing. Joint ventures are governed by a written operating agreement defining contributions, distributions, and exit. We also invest our own capital in every project.
Do you work outside Massachusetts?
Our core market is Malden and Greater Boston, with active interest across Middlesex County and select Northeast markets. We only operate where we have contractor relationships and real local knowledge — we don't buy remotely on guesswork.
I'm an agent with a property to refer. How does that work?
Reach out through the contact page with the address and situation. If we close on a referred property, we discuss referral terms upfront in writing — no ambiguity, no handshake deals.
